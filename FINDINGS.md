## The One-Line Summary

Olist is growing fast — but almost entirely on the back of new customers, while retention, delivery reliability, and seller quality remain unresolved risks that could derail that growth.

---

## 1. Revenue Is Growing, But For the Wrong Reason

Revenue grew **41.5% in the last six months** compared to the previous six months, reaching ₹6.29M. On the surface this looks like a healthy business. But when you decompose the growth, average order value has stayed flat at ₹133 throughout — meaning every rupee of growth is coming from acquiring new customers, not from existing ones spending more.

This is an expensive and fragile growth model. If acquisition slows — due to competition, marketing spend cuts, or market saturation — there is no retention base to fall back on.

**Business implication**: The platform needs to shift focus from pure acquisition to building repeat purchase behaviour. A customer who buys twice is worth far more than two customers who each buy once.

---

## 2. Customer Retention Is at Crisis Level

Of 93,358 customers, **97% purchased only once**. The average customer placed just 1.03 orders in their entire lifetime on the platform. Month-over-month, fewer than 1 in 100 customers returned the following month.

This is not a normal churn rate — it suggests customers are not finding enough reason to come back. Notably, the customers who *do* return give measurably higher ratings, confirming that good experience drives loyalty. The problem is not the product category — it is the post-purchase experience.

**Business implication**: Loyalty programs, personalised re-engagement campaigns, and post-delivery follow-ups could have an outsized impact here given how low the baseline is. Even moving retention from 3% to 6% would materially change the revenue trajectory.

---

## 3. Delivery Performance Is Hurting Customer Satisfaction

**8% of all orders are delivered late**, with an average delay of 9.4 days among late orders. Analysis of delay against customer ratings reveals a sharp drop in satisfaction once delays exceed 5 days — the 6 to 30 day window has the highest concentration of both delayed orders and low ratings.

The problem is not evenly distributed. Specific product categories — Cool Stuff, Sports & Leisure, and Computer Accessories — account for a disproportionate share of delays and have the lowest average ratings (~2.5 out of 5). Within those categories, a small number of sellers are responsible for the majority of the delay.

**Business implication**: A targeted intervention on the 10–15 worst-performing sellers in high-delay categories would likely yield more improvement than a platform-wide logistics overhaul. The 5-day threshold should be formalised as an internal SLA.

---

## 4. A Small Group of Sellers Is a Brand Risk

Of 2,965 active sellers, only **56.5% meet basic quality benchmarks** (rating above 4, delay within normal range). More concerning: **47 sellers who generate above-average revenue are rated below 3.5 out of 5**.

These sellers are generating significant transaction volume while simultaneously creating negative customer experiences. Because they are high-revenue, they likely receive implicit preferential treatment — but they are actively eroding platform trust at scale.

**Business implication**: High revenue should not exempt sellers from quality standards. These 47 sellers warrant immediate audit. Mid-tier sellers with strong ratings should be promoted through visibility incentives to reduce platform dependency on a small group of underperformers.

## Summary of Recommendations

| Priority | Area | Action |
|---|---|---|

| 🔴 High | Seller Quality | Audit 47 high-revenue, low-rating sellers immediately |

| 🟡 Medium | Revenue Quality | Invest in upsell and cross-sell to grow AOV alongside volume |
